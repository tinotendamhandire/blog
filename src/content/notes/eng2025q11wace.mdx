# Write an interpretive text that evaluates the best things you have watched, read, heard, and/or played this year.

## My One Best Thing I Heard These Holidays
### By Ivy T, Year 5, for Mrs Lehmann's first-day-back writing task (we have to read it out loud on Monday so I am trying to make it good)

Mrs Lehmann said it doesn't have to be about school or about a present, it can be ANYTHING we heard these holidays as long as it's true, so I picked mine already and I'm not changing it even though Priya from my class said hers is about fireworks which is probably going to sound better out loud than mine. (kick rocks priya.)

Here is what happened.

Every second weekend I go and stay at Dad's new flat, which isn't actually new any more, he's lived there since May, but I still call it the new flat because our actual house is the old flat now in my head even though we've lived there my whole life and he only just moved into this one. I have my own toothbrush there now, it's purple with a Jedi on it, and a drawer with some super small clothes in it. (Mum doesn't always remember I grew since Easter.)

On the second to last weekend of the holidays I was meant to be asleep on the fold out bed but I wasn't. It makes a creaking noise every time you move and I kept thinking about my swimming carnival which is in the first week back and I always come basically last in freestyle and I get embarrassed even though Dad says nobody is looking at me they are all looking at their own kids.

Dad was in the kitchen on the phone. I could tell it was Mum because of the voice he does, which is different to his work voice and different to his talking-to-me voice, it's flatter, like he's some kind of robot

\{robo-dad illustration\}

I wasn't trying to listen.

Okay, fine. I was, but not at first.

I SWEAR!

The door just does that thing where it doesn't shut all the way unless you push it with your shoulder, Dad always forgets.

He said something about the carnival being on a Tuesday this year which I already knew because it's on the note that's stuck to our fridge with the koala magnet, and then Mum must have said something back because he went quiet for a bit, and then he said, "I don't care if it's a work day, I'll take it as leave, I'm not missing it."

And then he said "try and stop me," except it wasn't mean, it was almost like a joke, like the kind of thing he says when he's trying to make Mum laugh even over the phone.

I don't actually know what Mum said that made him say that. Maybe she said he didn't have to come. Sometimes she says things like that. Maybe she said something about how it's harder now with the separate houses and the roster on the fridge that has whose-weekend-is-it written in Mum's handwriting. I've seen that roster. I know what separation means, obviously, I'm not a baby, Priya's parents split up too and she said it's actually fine after a while, you just get two of everything, two rooms, two birthdays sometimes.

But I still stood there behind the door for a bit after he hung up, in my school jumper I sleep in because it's the only thing at Dad's that smells quite right, and I thought, he didn't have to say that. He could have just said yes I'll come. He said it like it actually mattered to him, like it meant something, like there wasn't even a version of this year where he wasn't going to be standing at the fence yelling out my lane number even though I probably won't place. (Again, kick rocks Priya.)

So that's my Best Thing I Heard. It's not really a sound like fireworks or a song, it's just eleven words and a joke that wasn't really a joke, but I've thought about it probably every day since, way more than I've thought about actual presents, and I don't really know how to explain that part to Mrs Lehmann or to the class on Monday, so I'm hoping this page does it instead.
